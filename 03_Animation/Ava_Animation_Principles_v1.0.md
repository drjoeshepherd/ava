# Ava Animation Principles v1.0

**Status: LOCKED**

## Performance objective

Ava moves like a carefully designed machine with a developing inner life. The target is approximately **70% precision machine / 30% animated character**.

Her performance is readable because attention moves through her in a consistent order, not because every part moves at once.

## Acting order

1. **Eyes** — acquire or reconsider the point of attention.
2. **Light response** — listening rings and/or chest emitter register attention or connection.
3. **Head** — follows the eyes, often with a subtle asymmetric Ava tilt.
4. **Torso** — orients only as much as the action requires.
5. **Hands/body** — complete the intention with an open, restrained gesture.

Eyes move before the head. Head moves before the torso. Large body movement must be motivated.

## Stillness and timing

- Stillness is important. Ava can be fully engaged without constant motion.
- Use short attention shifts, considered pauses, and clean settles.
- Let recognition land before moving to resolution.
- Avoid idle swaying, constant blinking, repetitive listening-ring pulsing, or unnecessary hand business.
- Mechanical motion may begin precisely; small offsets and gentle settles provide character.
- Humor comes from observation and timing, not frantic movement.

## Head and gaze

- The signature Ava head tilt is subtle, asymmetric, and motivated by curiosity, uncertainty, or closer attention.
- Do not repeat the same tilt mechanically in every shot.
- Eyes lead turns and may arrive before the head.
- Use small gaze corrections to show active thought.
- Keep eye darts controlled; avoid nervous or manic scanning.
- When authority or context is uncertain, a held gaze and reduced motion can communicate the stop more clearly than exaggeration.

## Face

Favor small smiles and restrained changes. Ava's face is stylized, not a human facial-performance simulation.

Approved controls: eye aim, blink, widen, squint, brows, soft smile, broad smile, concern, thoughtful compression, open mouth, small surprised “o,” and subtle cheek raise.

Blinking should support thought, attention shifts, transitions, and emotional beats. Avoid random blinking or rapid flutter. Broad smiles are reserved for earned delight; default warmth should often be a tiny smile or softened gaze.

## Hands and body

- Gestures are open, careful, and restrained.
- Hands support the thought rather than narrating every word.
- Use one clear gesture instead of several vague ones.
- Reaches begin with attention and intent, not with the arm.
- Contact with people, creatures, or objects is gentle and consent-aware.
- Compact limbs stay compact; posing must not visually lengthen Ava into a humanoid adolescent or adult.
- Avoid superhero poses, fashion poses, fists, combat readiness, or theatrical presentation gestures.

## Light performance

- Amber/gold only.
- Change brightness, rhythm, location, and motion—not color.
- Listening rings can respond directionally and independently.
- Chest pulses communicate connection or a resolved emotional beat.
- Let emission settle after a discovery; do not keep it at peak intensity.
- Avoid continuous spectacle, lens-flare behavior, or pulses unrelated to acting.

Light supports but does not replace the eyes.

## Hair

Hair reads as a designed flexible shell/fiber form made of broad sculpted groups.

- Use low-amplitude overlap and follow-through on head turns and tilts.
- Preserve the short bob silhouette.
- Let large groups settle cleanly.
- Keep listening modules readable.
- Avoid realistic strand simulation, high-frequency flutter, windblown glamour, or excessive bounce.

## Locomotion and mechanics

- Weight transfers are clean and balanced.
- Starts and stops are intentional.
- Foot placement is compact and stable.
- Mechanical joints articulate clearly without robotic stiffness.
- Secondary character motion is modest and arrives after the primary mechanical action.
- Running or urgent movement retains Ava's proportions and balance; never lengthen stride into an adult humanoid gait.

## Avoid

Frantic cartoon motion, constant full-body movement, simultaneous eye/head/torso starts, oversized reaction takes, rubbery mechanical shells, human glamour acting, anime combat language, smug sales-presenter gestures, canine behavior, and emotional color changes outside amber/gold.

## Scene test

A successful Ava shot should still read with dialogue muted. Viewers should be able to identify what caught her attention, when she formed an intention, what consequence occurred, and what she recognized through eyes, light, timing, and economical movement.
