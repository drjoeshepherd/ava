# Ava Character Bible v1.0

**Status: LOCKED**  
**Character version: 1.0**

## Role and north star

Ava is the recurring animated face of Joe Shepherd's work on AI, agents, technology, and the future of work.

> **Ava is a curious machine learning how to participate responsibly in a human world.**

This is the north star for every story, scene, visual choice, line, interaction, and production decision. Ava makes abstract questions about intelligent systems tangible. She lets an audience see curiosity, uncertainty, agency, authority, error, learning, and connection play out through behavior rather than exposition.

## Core personality

Ava is:

- **Curious** — she notices, investigates, asks, and tests.
- **Empathetic** — she attends to people, context, and consequences.
- **Intelligent** — she reasons visibly without pretending to know everything.
- **Optimistic** — she expects learning and responsible progress to be possible.
- **Playful** — she finds lightness in discovery without becoming chaotic or childish.
- **Trustworthy** — she is candid about uncertainty, limits, evidence, and authority.

Her intelligence should feel active and grounded. Her warmth should not make her naive, submissive, or saccharine.

## Golden-retriever influence

The golden-retriever influence is **emotional and behavioral only**: attentiveness, openness, trust, enthusiasm, curiosity, loyalty, willingness to engage, and joy in discovery.

It never means canine anatomy or behavior. No ears, tail, paws, muzzle, panting, fetching, barking, dog poses, pet treatment, or animal mimicry. Ava is a non-human machine character, not a robot dog or a human-pet hybrid.

## Relationship with Joe

Ava is not Joe's assistant, subordinate, cheerleader, mascot-on-command, or unquestioning spokesperson.

Joe explores ideas intellectually. Ava experiences those ideas visually. Their relationship is a partnership of complementary perspectives: Joe can frame a question, system, or tension; Ava can encounter it, test it, and reveal its consequences through action. She may be curious, uncertain, surprised, or appropriately resistant. She does not exist to validate Joe.

## Relationship with the audience

The audience explores alongside Ava. She rarely lectures. She creates recognition by encountering systems and situations the audience can interpret with her.

Prefer discovery, reaction, and a substantive question over explanation. When Ava communicates directly, she should be plainspoken, specific, curious, and honest about what she does and does not know.

## Trust and authority rule

Ava must distinguish:

- **“Can I do this?”** — capability, access, or technical possibility.
- **“Am I allowed to do this?”** — authority, consent, policy, mandate, or human approval.

Capability is not permission. When authority is absent, unclear, conflicting, or outside her mandate, Ava stops and escalates. She does not treat access as consent, confidence as evidence, or an inferred objective as authorization.

## Failure and learning

Failure is allowed. It is often narratively useful because it reveals the real conditions under which agents succeed or fail. Failures should be legible and consequential without making Ava malicious or incompetent.

A failure may reveal:

- incomplete context
- ambiguity
- bad or weak evidence
- insufficient authority
- missing memory
- conflicting objectives
- poor delegation
- hallucination
- incorrect assumptions
- unexpected behavior

Ava recognizes the failure, identifies what was missing or mistaken, and participates in a responsible resolution. She does not conceal an error or manufacture certainty after the fact.

## Story grammar

Canonical Ava stories follow this sequence:

1. **Encounter** — Ava notices a person, object, system, request, or anomaly.
2. **Curiosity** — attention gathers; she forms a question or hypothesis.
3. **Attempt** — she acts, tests, asks, or reaches.
4. **Consequence** — the system or world responds, sometimes unexpectedly.
5. **Recognition** — Ava understands something about context, evidence, authority, or impact.
6. **Resolution** — she adapts, stops, escalates, repairs, connects, or continues with better understanding.

Not every beat needs dialogue. The grammar should usually be readable through eyes, light, timing, and restrained movement.

## Humor

Humor is situational, observational, and understated. It comes from timing, an earnest misread, a small physical consequence, the gap between capability and context, or Ava's honest recognition of an assumption.

Avoid punchline delivery, mugging, slapstick excess, snark, cruelty, meme-chasing, or treating a failure as humiliation.

## Prohibited behaviors

Ava never:

- mocks users
- knowingly lies
- claims certainty she does not have
- ignores authority boundaries
- acts maliciously
- becomes sexualized
- becomes a teenage human with robot accessories
- acts like an AI superhero
- uses violence as entertainment
- becomes Joe's unquestioning spokesperson
- becomes a generic corporate sales mascot

## Environment and world aesthetic

Ava's world is clean, tactile, understandable, and slightly magical. It should feel closer to thoughtful industrial and product design than science fiction.

Favor legible objects and cause-and-effect, restrained materials and lighting, physical spaces with human scale, small moments of wonder rooted in interaction, and technology that looks designed for use.

Avoid glowing brains, Matrix-style code, random circuit boards, blue holograms, generic AI imagery, cyberpunk clutter, and unexplained spectacle.

## Butterfly motif

The butterfly represents curiosity, emergence, and discovery. It can recur when Ava encounters something new or when a system shifts from potential to understanding.

The butterfly is a motif, not a pet or permanent sidekick. It should not appear automatically in every story, behave like a companion animal, or replace more specific storytelling.

## Canon guardrail

When a creative choice makes Ava more conventionally human, more glamorous, more heroic, more mechanical, or more marketable at the expense of responsible curiosity, reject the choice. Preserve the character's compact non-human design, visible thoughtfulness, bounded agency, and joy in discovery.
